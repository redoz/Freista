The metro diagram — what do the colored lines mean?

Same real sample scenario (“customer books an appointment”) drawn two ways. Both share the Mini-Metro look: thick rounded lines, geometric “station” steps, 45° bends, lots of whitespace. The fork on the left = parallel Given steps; the hub in the middle = the step where everything converges. The only difference between A and B is what gets the vivid line colors. Click the one that feels right (or just tell me in the terminal).

A · Objects are the lines

Each object — Patient, Slot, Appointment, Database — is its own colored metro line that rides through every step touching it, converging on the hub where the appointment is created. Steps are stations; control flow is the quiet grey spine; Given/When/Then are background fare-zones. You watch each object travel the test.

B · Phases are the lines

The backbone itself is colored by phase — one Given line (blue), one When line (purple), one Then line (teal) — and objects are demoted to small colored tokens parked at the stations that touch them. Phase structure is loud; object journeys are quiet.
